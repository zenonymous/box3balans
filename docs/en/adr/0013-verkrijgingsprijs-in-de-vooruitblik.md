# The preview uses its own acquisition price, not the cost basis

_Nederlands: [0013-verkrijgingsprijs-in-de-vooruitblik.md](../../adr/0013-verkrijgingsprijs-in-de-vooruitblik.md)_

Under the amendment (novelle, Kamerstuk 37 053, November 2026; not law yet), financial instruments are taxed on their capital gain (vermogenswinst) from the start of the new system, proposed for 1 January 2029: the proceeds minus the acquisition price (verkrijgingsprijs). For what you already had at the start, that acquisition price is its value on that day; after that, it's the average price of everything you hold of the same asset, as in box 2. The cost basis in the app is something else: what you actually paid, per account, and optionally FIFO. So the preview works out its own acquisition price from the transactions: per asset and per owner across all of that owner's accounts, starting from the value at the start, always averaged, whatever the cost basis setting is. A transfer between your own accounts doesn't change it. The preview therefore differs from _Results_, but follows the law.

Whether an asset is a financial instrument depends on where it's held, not only on its asset class. Shares, funds and ETFs always are. Crypto and precious metal are only when held at a broker: an ETP or ETC is a financial instrument. That's our reading, since the explanatory memorandum doesn't mention them. The asset class _other_ also counts only at a broker (bonds, structured products); elsewhere it's things like money lent to others. Cash is never a financial instrument, not even at a broker. Coins in a wallet or on an exchange, precious metal in a vault or at home, bank balances and the rest stay on capital accrual (vermogensaanwas) in 2029, as the amendment proposes.

How the acquisition price is worked out, in detail:

- **Costs:** the costs of buying are part of the acquisition price, and the costs of selling come off the proceeds (art. 5.22 and 5.23). Once the preview adds the capital gain to a year's result, those costs must not also count as that year's deductible costs. Costs from before the start play no part: the value at the start replaces everything paid before.
- **Regular benefits:** dividend, interest and other rewards count gross, in the year you get them. Tax withheld abroad doesn't make them smaller. Units you receive as a reward come in at their value on that day.
- **Leaving without a sale:** units you withdraw or send to someone else take their share of the acquisition price along, with no gain. Whether the law will treat that as a sale isn't clear yet.
- **Selling more than you hold:** only the part you hold counts, with a warning. A purchase is missing in that case.
- **No value at the start:** an asset you held at the start without a known value that day is left out and named. Counting it as bought for €0 would tax its whole value as gain.
- **Rounding:** the preview keeps the acquisition price of all units of an asset together, not a price per unit in cents, so very cheap units don't round to €0.

**Joint accounts** (decided on 9 November 2026): a joint account is split by each partner's share into each partner's own average. Each partner holds their share of the joint account's units, at that share of its acquisition price, and those units join the same asset from that partner's own accounts in one average. A sale from the joint account is split the same way, and so is its gain. Example: you own 60% of a joint account with 10 units and have 4 of your own, all worth €100 at the start: 10 units at €1,000 for you, 4 at €400 for your partner. The joint account buys 10 at €200: you now have 16 for €2,200 (€137.50 each), your partner 8 for €1,200 (€150 each). It sells 10 at €180: your gain is 6 × (180 − 137.50) = €255, your partner's 4 × (180 − 150) = €120.

Not decided yet: accounts kept as values per year have no sales, so no capital gain can be worked out for them. They can stay on capital accrual with a message, or get a field for what was sold that year ([#11](https://github.com/zenonymous/box3balans/issues/11)).
